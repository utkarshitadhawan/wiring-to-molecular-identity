
The analyses provide evidence that connectivity architecture contains
predictive information about neurotransmitter identity within the examined
Drosophila olfactory neuron population.

The strongest predictive contributions were associated with features describing
synaptic-weight distributions, particularly outgoing mean and median synaptic
strength and incoming maximum synaptic strength. This suggests that the
distribution and strength of synaptic connections may carry molecularly
informative structure beyond simple measures of network size.

Feature-family analysis further indicates that outgoing and incoming
connectivity characteristics contributed more strongly than the simple
directionality ratios examined here.

However, these findings are associative rather than mechanistic. They do not
demonstrate that connectivity causes neurotransmitter identity, that
neurotransmitter identity can be universally inferred from connectivity, or
that the identified features are independently causal.

The result should therefore be interpreted as a computational
proof-of-concept: within this olfactory dataset, neural wiring contains
measurable information that can be used to predict a molecular property of
neurons.
